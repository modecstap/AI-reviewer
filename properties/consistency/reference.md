# Logical coherence and consistency

Criterion handbook. Assessment procedure: see `instruction.md`.

The sample corrections below show how the connection between steps is restored. In a correction to the article under review, take the missing link, measurement, and cause only from that article.

## Definition

**Logical coherence and consistency** is a structure of the article in which the problem statement, the analysis of existing approaches, the argumentation, the results, and the conclusions form a traceable chain. Each claim is connected to the previous one; the parts of the text relate to one research task and do not contradict each other without explanation. The conclusions follow from the argumentation already presented and do not introduce propositions that the main body has not prepared.

Consistency is not reduced to the formal order of sections. If the connection remains only in the author's intention and is not expressed in the text, this is a logical gap.

## Assessment questions

| Feature | Question | Violation if |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trajectory | Is the movement from the problem to approaches, arguments, results, and conclusions visible? | no |
| Connection between claims | Does each next claim follow from the previous one? | no |
| Unity of task | Do the sections serve one stated task? | no |
| Leap | Is there a conclusion whose grounds are omitted from the text? | yes |
| Non-contradiction | Does one section, without explanation, cancel what another asserts? | yes |
| Boundary of conclusions | Do propositions appear in the conclusion that were not in the main body? | yes |
| Match to the question | Do the results answer the question that was posed? | no |
| Order of grounds | Do the grounds for choosing a method or criterion come before the conclusion that rests on them? | no |

Genre and the publication's requirements may change the composition. What does not change: the line of reasoning can be traced.

## Violation types

### 1. Conclusion before the problem statement

Features: trajectory, order of grounds.

Error: a recommendation comes before the problem, the subject, and the grounds are named. The reader does not yet know what question the article addresses.

Inconsistent:

> The proposed method should be implemented in the educational process. In recent years, researchers have been discussing how digital tools affect students' independent work.

Correction:

> In recent years, researchers have been discussing how digital tools affect students' independent work. This article examines this problem using material from a single course. Based on the results of the course, the conditions under which the tool examined can be used in a similar educational process are formulated below.

Rule: first the problem and the limits of the material, then what kind of conclusion is possible. Do not move a recommendation for which the article does not yet have grounds to the beginning as one already accepted.

### 2. Logical leap between claims

Features: leap, connection between claims.

Error: a link between the premise and the conclusion is missing. The stated consequence does not follow from the stated indicator.

Inconsistent:

> In a sample of 40 students, the average time spent preparing for a class increased by 15 minutes. Therefore, digital technologies improve the quality of education.

Correction:

> In a sample of 40 students, the average time spent preparing for a class increased by 15 minutes. This indicator describes the duration of preparation and does not by itself allow one to judge the quality of education. Such a conclusion requires the results by which quality was measured in this study.

Rule: show which conclusion does not follow from this indicator. Do not add a measurement that is not in the article. Remove a conclusion without grounds.

### 3. Literature review does not serve the task

Features: unity of task.

Error: the information may be correct in itself but is not a step toward the stated task.

Inconsistent:

> The task of the study is to compare two methods of feedback on written assignments. The history of writing spans several millennia and is linked to the development of states, law, and school education.

Correction:

> The task of the study is to compare two methods of feedback on written assignments: a summary comment and step-by-step remarks along the text. Below we review studies in which these methods have already been compared in terms of revision quality and the time the instructor spends on checking.

Rule: keep in the review the approaches that are needed for the stated comparison. Remove a topic that does not serve the task. Do not add works the article does not name: in that case, write in the problem that the review is not connected to the task.

### 4. Results answer a different question

Features: match to the question.

Error: the result obtained relates to a different subject than the question posed.

Inconsistent:

> The study was intended to establish whether the number of factual errors decreases after using a checklist. According to the survey, 78% of participants reported that they liked the checklist.

Correction:

> The study was intended to establish whether the number of factual errors decreases after using a checklist. In texts checked with the checklist, the average number of such errors was 4.1 per paper; in texts without the checklist — 6.7. Separately, 78% of participants reported that they liked the checklist; this response relates to the perception of the tool, not to the number of errors.

Rule: place each result next to the question it answers. If the article contains no answer to the stated question, do not invent numbers. Keep the adjacent observation and name its subject. In the problem, write that the answer to the posed question is missing.

### 5. Contradiction between sections

Features: non-contradiction.

Error: the conclusion cancels the result without explaining the discrepancy.

Inconsistent:

> The results section states that the differences between the groups did not reach statistical significance. The conclusions state: "The experiment confirmed the advantage of the experimental method."

Correction:

> The differences between the groups did not reach statistical significance. The data obtained do not confirm the advantage of the experimental method; they only allow one to describe the direction of the differences and to indicate the need for a repeated test on a larger sample.

Rule: the conclusion preserves what is shown in the results. If the discrepancy is discussed in the article, do not add it to the list.

### 6. Circular reasoning

Features: connection between claims, leap.

Error: the second sentence repeats the first and adds neither a criterion nor an independent observation.

Inconsistent:

> The method is effective because it gives a good result. The good result is explained by the fact that the method is effective.

Correction:

> In this study, the effectiveness of the method was assessed by the share of tasks completed without factual errors. In the experimental group this share was 71%, in the control group — 54%. It is by this indicator that the method gave a higher result under the conditions of the class conducted.

Rule: define the concept through a separate indicator and comparison that the article already contains. If there is no indicator, break the circle: remove the mutual explanation and write in the problem which criterion is missing.

### 7. New claim only in the conclusions

Features: boundary of conclusions.

Error: the conclusion introduces a proposition that the main body has not prepared.

Inconsistent:

> Thus, the study showed an increase in the accuracy of answers. Moreover, the data obtained prove that the same technique improves long-term retention after six months.

Correction:

> Thus, the study showed an increase in the accuracy of answers immediately after the class. The effect of the same technique on retention after six months was not established from the data presented and requires a separate test.

Rule: in the conclusion, keep only the consequence that the main body already contains. If delayed data are in the article but appear only in the conclusions, write in the problem that they belong in the main body, and do not present them as an already proven outcome.

### 8. Broken order of justification

Features: order of grounds.

Error: a consequence comes before the grounds on which it rests.

Inconsistent:

> The selected statistical test confirmed the differences between the groups. For this purpose, the groups were first checked for normality of distribution, after which Student's t-test was selected.

Correction:

> The distribution of the indicators in both groups was checked for normality. Since the normality condition was met, Student's t-test was used to compare the means. This test showed differences between the groups.

Rule: first the grounds for the choice, then the test itself, then its result. Do not add new checks.

## Acceptable progression

Logical coherence does not require a single five-step scheme. Do not add such a fragment to the list if the line of reasoning can be traced.

Inconsistent:

> The result is named before it is explained which question it answers.

Consistent:

> The question is posed, the method of answering is stated, the result is attributed specifically to this question.

## Markers

Check these words first. A word by itself is not a violation: the questions and the type decide.

- Leap: therefore, thus, moreover, proven — if the premise is not in the text.
- Premature conclusion: should be implemented, confirmed — before the question is posed or before the result.
- Circle: because, is explained by — if the second sentence only repeats the first.
- Usually keep: this indicator does not allow one to judge, the response relates to, the data do not confirm, was not established from the data presented, since the condition was met.
