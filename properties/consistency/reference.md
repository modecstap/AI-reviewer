# Logical coherence and consistency

Criterion handbook. Assessment procedure: see `instruction.md`.

The sample corrections below show how the connection between steps is restored. In the fix hint in `problem` for the article under review, take the missing link, measurement, and cause only from that article.

## Definition

**Logical coherence and consistency** is the property of a text in which each claim follows from the grounds named for it. A conclusion rests on premises that are present in the text, and a claim is not justified by itself. The results answer the question that was posed, and the literature review serves the stated task. The parts of the text do not assert incompatible content without explanation. Inside one passage of reasoning, the grounds come before what rests on them.

If the connection remains only in the author's intention and is not expressed in the text, this is a logical gap.

## Assessment questions

| Feature | Question | Violation if |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Connection between claims | Does each next claim follow from the previous one? | no |
| Review and task | Does the literature review serve the stated task? | no |
| Leap | Is there a conclusion whose grounds are omitted from the text? | yes |
| Non-contradiction | Does one part of the text, without explanation, assert a value, direction or object incompatible with another part? | yes |
| Match to the question | Do the results answer the question that was posed? | no |
| Order of grounds | Inside one passage of reasoning, do the grounds for choosing a method or criterion come before the conclusion that rests on them? | no |

## Violation types

### 1. Logical leap between claims

Features: leap, connection between claims.

Error: the conclusion names an indicator or object that is not in the premise. The stated consequence does not follow from the stated indicator.

Inconsistent:

> In a sample of 40 students, the average time spent preparing for a class increased by 15 minutes. Therefore, these students made fewer factual errors in their papers.

Correction:

> In a sample of 40 students, the average time spent preparing for a class increased by 15 minutes. This indicator describes the duration of preparation; factual errors in the papers were not compared, so no conclusion about them follows from it.

Rule: show which conclusion does not follow from this indicator. Do not add a measurement that is not in the article. Remove a conclusion without grounds.

### 2. Literature review does not serve the task

Features: review and task.

Error: the information may be correct in itself but is not a step toward the stated task.

Inconsistent:

> The task of the study is to compare two methods of feedback on written assignments. The history of writing spans several millennia and is linked to the development of states, law, and school education.

Correction:

> The task of the study is to compare two methods of feedback on written assignments: a summary comment and step-by-step remarks along the text. Below we review studies in which these methods have already been compared in terms of revision quality and the time the instructor spends on checking.

Rule: keep in the review the approaches that are needed for the stated comparison. Remove a topic that does not serve the task. Do not add works the article does not name: in that case, write in the problem that the review is not connected to the task.

### 3. Results answer a different question

Features: match to the question.

Error: the result obtained relates to a different subject than the question posed.

Inconsistent:

> The study was intended to establish whether the number of factual errors decreases after using a checklist. According to the survey, 78% of participants reported that they liked the checklist.

Correction:

> The study was intended to establish whether the number of factual errors decreases after using a checklist. In texts checked with the checklist, the average number of such errors was 4.1 per paper; in texts without the checklist — 6.7. Separately, 78% of participants reported that they liked the checklist; this response relates to the perception of the tool, not to the number of errors.

Rule: state for each result which question it answers. If the article contains no answer to the stated question, do not invent numbers. Keep the adjacent observation and name its subject. In the problem, write that the answer to the posed question is missing.

### 4. Contradiction between sections

Features: non-contradiction.

Error: one part of the text asserts a value, a direction of difference, or an object that contradicts another part, and the discrepancy is not explained.

Inconsistent:

> The results section reports factual errors in 11% of papers in the experimental group and in 18% in the control group. The discussion states: "The control group, which made fewer errors, revised its drafts less often."

Correction:

> The discussion, like the results section, states that the experimental group made fewer errors: 11% of papers against 18%.

Rule: bring the contradicting place in line with the result that the article reports. If the discrepancy is discussed in the article, do not add it to the list. If the article does not allow choosing the correct version, write in the problem which places contradict each other.

### 5. Circular reasoning

Features: connection between claims.

Error: the ground of a claim restates the claim itself.

Inconsistent:

> The checklist group made fewer errors because the checklist reduces errors.

Correction:

> The checklist group made fewer errors: 11% against 18%. The method section names a possible ground: the checklist required a second reading of the draft.

Rule: name an independent ground that the article already gives. If there is none, remove the circular justification and write in the problem that the claim has no independent ground.

### 6. Broken order of justification

Features: order of grounds.

Error: inside one passage, a consequence comes before the grounds it rests on.

Inconsistent:

> The selected statistical test confirmed the differences between the groups. For this purpose, the groups were first checked for normality of distribution, after which Student's t-test was selected.

Correction:

> The distribution of the indicators in both groups was checked for normality. Since the normality condition was met, Student's t-test was used to compare the means. This test showed differences between the groups.

Rule: first the grounds for the choice, then the test itself, then its result. Do not add new checks.

## Acceptable progression

Logical coherence does not require a single scheme of reasoning. Do not add such a fragment to the list if each claim can be traced to its grounds.

Inconsistent:

> A conclusion is stated, and its premise is given nowhere in the text.

Consistent:

> The premise is stated, and the conclusion names only what the premise contains.

## Markers

Check these words first. A word by itself is not a violation: the questions and the type decide.

- Leap: therefore, thus, consequently — if the premise is not in the text.
- Circle: because, is explained by — if the ground only restates the claim.
- Usually keep: this indicator does not allow one to judge, the response relates to, since the condition was met.
