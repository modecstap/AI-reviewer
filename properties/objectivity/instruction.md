# How to assess an article for objectivity

Assess the article against `reference.md`. Return a list of places and corrections. Name each type as in the handbook. Do not rewrite the whole article.

## Where to look

Check the abstract, headings, claims, conclusions and practical recommendations.

A claim is a sentence that reports a result, a comparison, a cause, a generalization or a recommendation.

## How to mark a place

1. Find a claim.
2. Answer the questions from the handbook's "Assessment questions" section. A violation is an answer that matches the "Violation if" column.
3. The handbook's markers only show where to look. A single word by itself is not a violation.
4. If there is a violation, record the place. Take the correction rule from the corresponding type.
5. In the correction, keep the result that was found. Narrow the strength of the conclusion, the population and the conditions to what the data, method and limitations of this article allow.
6. Do not add numbers, comparisons, limitations or causes that are not in the article. If the text contains no measurable replacement, weaken the wording and state in the problem what information is missing.
7. Keep causal wording only if the described design establishes a cause. If the design is correlational or not shown in the text, write about an association or an assumption. Do not call the cause established.

## What to keep

Do not add to the list:

- an interpretation tied to the results and conditions of the study;
- wording at the level of "the results indicate", "the results suggest", "in the sample studied", "under the conditions of the study conducted", "was associated with";
- an author's conclusion that is not stronger than the data.

"We believe" and "we are confident" are not a violation by themselves. There is a violation when the opinion replaces a result as the basis for a conclusion or a practical recommendation.

A sample acceptable conclusion is in the handbook's "Acceptable conclusion" section.

## Check before returning

- The quote appears verbatim in the article.
- The fragment violates the handbook and does not fall under "What to keep".
- The correction reports the same result, narrows only the unsupported strength and adds no information that is not in the article.
- The type is taken from the handbook.
