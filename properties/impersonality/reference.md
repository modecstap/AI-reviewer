# Impersonality and restraint of presentation

Criterion handbook. Assessment procedure: see `instruction.md`.

The sample corrections below show the required focus of the sentence. In the fix hint in `problem` for the article under review, take numbers and comparisons only from that article.

## Definition

**Impersonality and restraint of presentation** is a way of constructing a sentence in which the material, procedure, analysis, and result are at the focus, and the register stays neutral and scientific. The pronoun "we" is acceptable for denoting completed actions, if this meets the publication's requirements, and does not replace the result with a conviction, colloquial or emotional vocabulary, an appeal to the reader, or the author's self-attestation.

## Assessment questions

| Feature | Question | Violation if |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sentence focus | Are the data, analysis, or procedure named, rather than the author's conviction? | no |
| "We" | Does "we" denote a researcher's action that is acceptable for the publication? | no |
| Vocabulary | Are there any colloquial, exclamatory, or emotionally charged words? | yes |
| Reader | Is there a demand to agree or an appeal to "everyone"? | yes |
| Polemic | Does the disagreement concern a thesis, method, or data of another work? | no |
| Course of work | Does the author's reaction replace the report of the result? | yes |
| Contribution | Is the contribution described through what the work does, rather than through the author's self-attestation or a priority claim? | no |

## Violation types

### 1. Personal conviction instead of a result

Features: sentence focus.

Error: the author's conviction stands at the focus of the sentence instead of the result being reported.

Unrestrained:

> I am convinced that papers checked against the checklist had fewer uncorrected factual errors: 11% against 18%.

Correction:

> The share of uncorrected factual errors was 11% in papers checked against the checklist and 18% in papers with only a final comment.

Rule: put at the focus the observation that the article reports. If no result stands behind the conviction, remove the conviction.

### 2. Colloquial or emotional evaluation

Features: vocabulary.

Error: a colloquial or emotionally charged word conveys the author's impression; the scientific register requires neutral words.

Unrestrained:

> The checklist turned out to be a real lifesaver: a stunning 24 of the 30 students corrected at least one flag.

Correction:

> With the checklist, 24 of the 30 students corrected at least one flagged factual error before submitting the final text.

Rule: replace colloquial and emotional words with neutral ones. Keep the facts that the sentence already contains.

### 3. "We" as the bearer of opinion

Features: "we", reader.

Error: "we believe" makes an opinion the basis, and the appeal to the reader demands agreement.

Unrestrained:

> We believe that the 11% against 18% speaks for itself, and we want the reader to finally acknowledge it.

Correction:

> The proportion of factual errors was 11% with the checklist and 18% with the final comment.

Rule: remove "we" where it does not name a research action. Remove the appeal to the reader.

### 4. Acceptable and excessive "we" in one passage

Features: "we", sentence focus.

Error: one "we" names an action, the other puts a conviction at the focus.

Unrestrained:

> We conducted a survey of 30 students. We are deeply convinced that their answers support our view.

Correction:

> We conducted a survey of 30 students. Their answers are summarized in Table 3.

Rule: keep the author's action. Remove the conviction and put the data at the focus.

### 5. Emotional polemic

Features: polemic, vocabulary.

Error: an assessment of people and annoyance stand in place of a thesis, method, or data of another work.

Unrestrained:

> Unfortunately, previous researchers never bothered to compare step-by-step checking with the final comment.

Correction:

> In the works of Ivanov (2016) and Orlova (2018), step-by-step checking was not compared with the final comment in terms of the proportion of factual errors. The present study supplies precisely this comparison.

Rule: shift the disagreement to the subject, method, or data. Do not add works or a gap that the article does not name: in that case, advise removing the assessment of people.

### 6. Rhetorical appeal

Features: reader, vocabulary.

Error: the audience's agreement, an exclamation, and an evaluative epithet stand in place of the result.

Unrestrained:

> Every teacher will agree: such a brilliant method can no longer be ignored!

Correction:

> The method considered is associated with a smaller number of uncorrected factual errors in one academic course. The decision to use it in other courses depends on whether the assignment type and the composition of errors match.

Rule: remove the appeal to "everyone", the exclamation and the evaluative epithet.

### 7. Colloquial account of the course of work

Features: course of work, vocabulary.

Error: the author's expectation and surprise add no information about the data.

Unrestrained:

> To be honest, we ourselves did not expect the share of errors to fall from 18% to 11% and were pleasantly surprised by the figures.

Correction:

> The proportion of factual errors decreased from 18% to 11%.

Rule: exclude the personal reaction. Keep the magnitude that the sentence already reports.

### 8. Self-presentation instead of a description of the contribution

Features: contribution.

Error: an unverifiable self-attestation of the author or a priority claim ("for the first time in science"). The formula "the author of these lines" by itself is merely stylistically heavy.

Unrestrained:

> The author of these lines has, for the first time in science, compared a step-by-step checklist with a final comment.

Correction:

> The paper compares a step-by-step checklist and a final comment on the material of one course.

Rule: describe the contribution through what the work does. Remove the self-attestation and the priority claim.

## Acceptable "we"

Restraint does not require hiding the person who made the observation. The boundary lies between action and assessment. Do not add such a passage to the list.

Acceptable:

> We excluded papers submitted after the deadline from the count.

Unrestrained:

> We were delighted with how wonderfully the remaining papers turned out.

If the publication requires an impersonal construction, the action is conveyed as follows: "Papers submitted after the deadline were excluded from the count." Only the grammar changes.

## Markers

Check these words first. A word by itself is not a violation: the questions and the type decide.

- Conviction instead of a result: I am convinced, we are sure, we believe, in my opinion, that we are right.
- Colloquial or emotional evaluation: just very, clearly a hit, to be honest, pleasantly surprised, brilliant, unfortunately, stunning, catastrophic, real lifesaver.
- Pressure on the reader: everyone will agree, can no longer, want the reader to, exclamation.
- Self-attestation of the author: for the first time in science, managed to solve, the author of these lines — if the sentence praises the author instead of saying what the work does.
- Usually keep: we conducted, we excluded, we limited, we coded, the analysis shows.
