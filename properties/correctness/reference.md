# Terminological correctness and precision of wording

Criterion handbook. Assessment procedure: see `instruction.md`.

The sample corrections below show the required match between word and concept and a verifiable wording. Take the term, quantity, period, source, and object name in a correction for the article under review only from that article. Do not add to the correction anything that is not in the article and does not follow from the text.

## Definition

**Terminological correctness and precision of wording** — the use of specialized vocabulary in the meaning accepted in the relevant scientific field, and the consistent, verifiable use of words in a scientific text. A term is needed to distinguish concepts, keeps one meaning throughout the text, and is explained if, without an explanation, its meaning in the given text is ambiguous. The objective, method, results, and conclusions name the same objects in the same way. Evaluations of degree, scope, and obviousness are replaced by an indication of the subject, measure, condition, period, or source, and pronouns and short references have a clear antecedent.

Excessive terminology, an everyday substitute for the needed term, and substitution of a closely related concept reduce precision equally. The academic quality of a text comes from the match between word and concept, not from the density of terms.

Precise wording does not have to contain every qualification in every sentence. A short term may be reused if its meaning has already been fixed.

## Assessment questions

| Feature | Question | Violation if |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Field | Is the accepted term in place of the specialized concept? | no |
| Meaning | Is the term replaced by a neighboring concept? | yes |
| Necessity | Does the rare word carry a distinct concept? | no |
| Consistency | Does the key word keep one meaning throughout the text? | no |
| Object name | Is one object called by one name from the objective to the conclusions, and different concepts by different names? | no |
| Explanation | Is an ambiguous or narrow term defined at its first substantive use? | no |
| Calque | Is it clear which procedure or scale is meant? | no |
| Audience | Does the degree of explanation match the genre and audience of the publication? | no |
| Method | Does the named method of analysis coincide with the one actually performed? | no |
| Measure | Is the evaluation of degree replaced by a quantity, criterion, or basis of comparison? | no |
| Comparison | Are the magnitude of a difference and statistical significance distinguished? | no |
| Scope | Are the population, period, condition, and composition of objects named? | no |
| Reference | Is it clear what "this", "the given", "the said" refer to? | no |
| Obviousness | Does "obviously" replace a definition, argument, or citation? | yes |
| Verifiability | Can one tell from the wording what exactly is being claimed? | no |

## Violation types

### 1. Everyday word instead of a term

Features: field.

Error: everyday descriptions are substituted for concepts that already exist in the work. It is unclear which objects and which indicators are meant.

Incorrect:

> We compared two groups of people and looked at how far the answers drifted apart on average.

Correction:

> Two independent samples were compared. For each, the mean value of the indicator and the standard deviation were calculated.

Rule: put the specialized word in place of a concept that the article already distinguishes. If the text does not allow choosing between the mean, the spread, and another indicator, do not replace the everyday description with a guess. In the problem, write which concept is not named.

### 2. Excessive string of terms

Features: necessity.

Error: a chain of rare terms does not convey the procedure. The reader does not learn what was done and with what material.

Incorrect:

> The cognitive-metacognitive construct was operationalized within the formative assessment paradigm by means of the explication of a checklist intervention.

Correction:

> One feature of independence was chosen for observation: whether the student corrects a marked factual error without a ready-made wording. This feature was recorded with a checklist at the draft stage.

Rule: remove rare terms where they do not add a distinct concept. Keep working terms that carry the procedure. Do not invent a procedure that the article does not describe: in that case, write in the problem that there is no action behind the string of terms.

### 3. Substitution of a closely related term

Features: meaning.

Error: closely related concepts that the field customarily distinguishes are mixed up in the text. Reliability concerns the stability of measurement. The correspondence of an instrument to the stated construct concerns validity.

Incorrect:

> The reliability of the test is confirmed by the fact that it measures precisely knowledge of factual material, not reading speed.

Correction:

> The content validity of the tasks was assessed by whether they relate to knowledge of factual material. The stability of results on repeated administration was not tested in this work, so no conclusion about the reliability of the test can be drawn from the data presented.

Rule: restore each concept to its own meaning. If the article had no repeated testing, do not attribute a conclusion about reliability to the text. Handle any other pair of closely related terms the same way: only when the confusion is visible from what the article itself claims about the procedure.

### 4. Terms from a different analytical operation

Features: method.

Error: one method is named, but the conclusion is attributed to another.

Incorrect:

> The correlation between the number of comments and the number of revisions was 0.46. Thus, the regression confirmed a moderate relationship.

Correction:

> The Pearson correlation coefficient between the number of comments and the number of revisions was 0.46. No regression analysis was performed in the work.

Rule: name the indicator that the article actually obtained. Remove the name of a method that is not in the work. If the article names both correlation and regression as performed procedures, keep both.

### 5. Ambiguous term without a fixed meaning

Features: explanation.

Error: the word refers to different objects in different fields, and the text does not fix the required meaning at the point where it can still be understood in two ways.

Incorrect:

> The article analyzes a feedback model.

Correction:

> Here, a feedback model means the teacher's sequence of actions: the moment of the comment, its subject, and the form of the student's response. A statistical model is not meant in this section.

Rule: fix the meaning with words that the rest of the article already allows you to choose. If no choice is possible, write in the problem which meanings compete, and do not fix one of them.

### 6. Term shifts meaning

Features: consistency.

Error: one word names different objects as the text proceeds. The reader cannot tell which of them is meant in the conclusion.

Incorrect:

> In the first part, the scheme of lesson stages is called the model. Later it says: "The model explains 42% of the variance." In the conclusions, the model is already the instructional scenario itself, which is recommended to schools.

Correction:

> The lesson scheme includes three stages. The regression model built from the lesson results explains 42% of the variance in the answer accuracy indicator. The conclusions concern not the regression model but the lesson scheme: transferring it to other schools requires separate testing.

Rule: give each meaning back its own name. Take the names from how the article itself describes the objects. If the meanings cannot be distinguished from the text, write in the problem which readings compete, and do not fix one of them.

### 7. Different names for one object

Features: object name, meaning.

Error: the objective, method, results, and conclusions use different words for one measured indicator, or neighboring terms are used as interchangeable although they denote different objects. A sample is not the same as a general population. Answer accuracy is not the same as academic success unless the latter is defined exactly that way. A new name broadens the result without a new observation.

Incorrect:

> The objective concerns the accuracy of factual information. The method measures literacy. The results report on text quality. The conclusions draw a conclusion about academic success.

Correction:

> All sections concern one indicator: the proportion of factual errors in a written paper. Literacy, overall text quality, and academic success were not measured in this article.

Incorrect:

> The sample of students showed an increase in accuracy. Consequently, this population is characterized by higher academic success.

Correction:

> In the surveyed sample, the proportion of answers without factual errors increased. Extending this observation to a broader population and to academic success in general would require a separate definition and a different sampling frame.

Rule: keep one name from the objective to the conclusions. Take the name from the indicator that the article actually measured. Do not use different terms as names for one result: leave a transfer to another concept or a broader population only where the article provides a definition and a frame. If the measurements are different, do not merge them into one object: write in the problem that the names diverge and which observation stands behind which name.

### 8. Inaccurate calque of a concept

Features: calque, explanation.

Error: the calque shows neither the moment of measurement nor the subject of the scale. The expression is not the name of a scale until the scale is specified.

Incorrect:

> Студенты прошли пре-тест и пост-тест на уровне знания.
> *(Gloss: "The students took a pre-test and a post-test at the knowledge level.")*

Correction:

> Before and after the lesson, the students took the same test consisting of fact-recall questions. Other levels of mastery, including application, were not tested separately.

Rule: name the procedure so that the moment of measurement and the subject of the test, already distinguishable from the article, are visible. Do not add levels that the article does not specify. If the subject of the test is unclear, do not replace the calque with a guess: write in the problem which scale is not named.

### 9. Narrow term without explanation where one is needed

Features: explanation, audience.

Error: the subsequent conclusion rests on the procedure, but the publication's reader cannot reconstruct what exactly the procedure does. The use of a narrow term by itself is not an error.

Incorrect:

> The calculation was performed using the bootstrap method with the Holm correction.

Correction:

> The confidence interval for the difference in means was constructed by bootstrapping: repeated random resampling from the obtained sample. Since three pairs of groups were compared, the Holm correction was applied to control for multiple comparisons.

Rule: keep the term and name its function in this work once, if the article already specifies that function. In a publication specialized in this procedure, do not require a repeated textbook definition. If the function is not named in the text, do not invent the number of comparisons or the object of estimation: write in the problem which function is not explained.

### 10. Evaluation without a measure or criterion

Features: measure, verifiability.

Error: degree words and evaluative characteristics name neither a criterion, nor a quantity, nor an indicator. It is unclear what exactly was observed.

Incorrect:

> The new checking method turned out to be very important and produced a fairly large gain in the quality of the papers.

Correction:

> After the step-by-step checklist was introduced, the proportion of papers without factual errors rose from 46% to 63%. Within this lesson, this gain refers to the accuracy of factual material, not to the assessment of style.

Incorrect:

> The algorithm is effective, convenient, and modern.

Correction:

> On the control set, the algorithm correctly classified 91% of objects with an average processing time of 0.4 s per object. Usability assessment was not part of the study.

Rule: replace the evaluation with an indicator, quantities, and the boundary of what was measured, if the article has them. Keep only measured properties. Do not add a property that was not in the study, and do not leave it as a characteristic. If there is no measure, remove the evaluation and write in the problem which measure is missing.

### 11. "Significantly" without a comparison

Features: comparison, measure.

Error: "significantly" can mean statistical significance, a large magnitude of difference, or a subjective assessment. These meanings do not coincide.

Incorrect:

> The experimental group showed a significantly higher result.

Correction:

> The mean score of the experimental group was 7.8, of the control group — 7.1, with the same maximum of 10. The difference is significant at the 0.05 level. The magnitude of the difference is 0.7 points.

Rule: separate the magnitude of the difference from statistical significance if the article reports both. If only one is reported, keep it and do not insert the other. If neither is reported, remove "significantly" and write in the problem which comparison is missing.

### 12. Unspecified scope

Features: scope.

Error: neither the set of sources, nor the list of indicators, nor the boundaries of the period, nor the quantity can be verified.

Incorrect:

> A number of authors note some increase in certain indicators in recent years.

Correction:

> Ivanov (2019) and Petrova (2021) record an increase in the proportion of independently completed assignments: from 38% in 2016 to 51% in 2020 in the student groups they surveyed. These works do not compare other indicators of success.

Rule: name the sources, indicator, period, and limit of generalization that the article already contains. If they are absent, do not fill "a number of", "some", and "in recent years" with a guess. In the problem, write which part of the scope is missing.

### 13. "Obviously" instead of grounds

Features: obviousness.

Error: "obviously" and the evaluative "optimal" close a question that requires grounds: why this threshold was chosen and with respect to what criterion it is optimal.

Incorrect:

> Obviously, the chosen threshold of 0.05 is optimal for this study.

Correction:

> For testing statistical hypotheses, the work adopts a significance level of 0.05. This threshold is set as a decision condition and by itself does not describe the effect size.

Rule: "obviously" does not replace a definition, argument, or citation. If the point is elementary and already justified in the article, keep the justification without the word "obviously". If there are no grounds, remove the word and write in the problem what is missing.

### 14. Unclear reference

Features: reference.

Error: "this" can be attributed to several preceding changes. It is not stated which of them was the criterion.

Incorrect:

> Teachers commented on spelling less often, and students revised paragraph composition more often. This confirms the effectiveness of the method.

Correction:

> If the effectiveness of the method is taken to be the proportion of paragraphs whose composition was revised after a comment, then the observed increase in such revisions is consistent with this criterion. The reduction in spelling comments does not relate to this criterion.

Rule: replace the reference with the named criterion if the article already specifies it. If the criterion is not specified, do not choose it yourself. In the problem, write which of the changes "this" may refer to.

## Acceptable usage

Keep the needed term and relate it to the concept once. Remove a word that carries no distinct meaning or stands in place of another, more precise one. Imprecision begins where a short word hides a different concept, a different measure, or a different scope. Do not add to the list a short term whose meaning has already been fixed.

Incorrect:

> On average, the answers drifted apart.

Also incorrect:

> To explicate the dispersion pattern of the answers.

where the standard deviation is meant.

Correct:

> The standard deviation was calculated.

Incorrect:

> The result improved noticeably.

Correct:

> The proportion of papers without factual errors rose from 46% to 63%.

## Markers

Check these words first. A word by itself is not a violation: the questions and the type decide.

- Everyday substitute: people, drifted apart, clicked, knowledge level — if the article already has a specialized concept.
- String without action: operationalization, construct, paradigm, explication in a row without a description of the step.
- Neighbor substitution: reliability instead of validity, correlation with a conclusion about regression, sample as population.
- Name shift: one key word in the objective, another in the method, a third in the conclusions.
- Calque: пре-тест ("pre-test"), пост-тест ("post-test"), форматирующее оценивание ("formative assessment") — if the procedure is not clear in Russian from the text.
- Degree without measure: very, fairly, significantly, noticeably, important, large, effective, convenient, modern, optimal.
- Unspecified scope: a number of, some, certain, in recent years, a number of authors.
- False obviousness: obviously, clearly, undoubtedly.
- Unclear reference: this, the given, the said — if there are several antecedents.
- Usually keep: a term defined once, the name of a performed method, an explanation of the function of a narrow procedure, an everyday word without a specialized concept, a quantity next to an evaluation, a significance level separate from the size of the difference, one name of an indicator across all sections.
